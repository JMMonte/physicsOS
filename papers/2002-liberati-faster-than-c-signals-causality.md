# Faster-than-c signals, special relativity, and causality

## One-line summary

Liberati, Sonego, and Visser separate "invariant speed" from "maximum speed" and show that constrained faster-than-c effects need not automatically cause causal loops.

## What it actually shows

- Special relativity's kinematic consistency requires an invariant speed; it does not by itself assume that no signal can exceed that speed.
- Causality conclusions depend on details of the FTL propagation law, especially whether a preferred frame or effective metric exists.
- The Scharnhorst effect is treated as a plausible, extremely tiny, constrained faster-than-c signal-speed modification.
- For a single Casimir-plate setup, the effective metric can be stably causal, preventing closed causal curves.
- The paper does not license arbitrary reciprocal FTL signaling devices. It is mainly a caution against sloppy "any FTL automatically breaks SR" statements.

## Methods (briefly)

Relativistic kinematics, effective-metric analysis, and causality checks for Scharnhorst-type photon propagation.

## Key equations / results

The paper's load-bearing point for this claim is conceptual: "faster than c" must specify propagation law and background. Unrestricted Lorentz-covariant FTL signaling and constrained effective-medium superluminality are different claims.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

- Special-relativistic kinematics plus specified FTL propagation models.
- Scharnhorst analysis assumes Casimir-boundary effective medium and is far below experimental verification.
- Does not address macroscopic spacecraft engineering.

## Caveats / open issues

This paper is sometimes misused in both directions. It does not prove FTL impossible; it also does not turn FTL into engineering. It says that causality analysis is model-specific.

## How it informs the FTL engineering claim

The paper is neutral-to-cautionary evidence. It supports keeping a loophole for constrained effective-metric effects, while undermining any broad inference from such effects to controllable macroscopic FTL transport.
