# Introducing Physical Warp Drives

## One-line summary

Bobrick and Martire generalize warp-drive spacetimes and find positive-energy subluminal classes, but they do not show how to accelerate a warp shell to superluminal speed.

## What it actually shows

- The paper reframes warp drives as shells of material enclosing a passenger region with modified spacetime.
- Positive-energy spherically symmetric warp drives are constructed for subluminal cases.
- Superluminal warp-drive solutions remain hypothetical in essentially the same sense as superluminal material objects.
- The authors state that there is no known way to accelerate regular material beyond `c`.
- They explicitly emphasize conservation of four-momentum and the absence, in the literature, of a full accelerating warp-drive metric for a realistic object.
- They do not support Lentz's claim of purely positive-energy superluminal drives; they say it needs further investigation.

## Methods (briefly)

Classical GR metric classification and stress-energy analysis for general warp-drive shells, including spherical and axisymmetric cases.

## Key equations / results

For the present claim, the important result is qualitative: positive-energy warp construction is obtained in subluminal cases; superluminal classes are not an established engineering route.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

- Classical GR.
- Idealized shells of regular or exotic material.
- Mostly kinematic/metric classification, not a formation or propulsion design.

## Caveats / open issues

The paper does keep "warp drive" research alive for subluminal and strong-gravity spacetime engineering. It does not establish FTL.

## How it informs the FTL engineering claim

This weighs against the user's phrasing. Even a pro-warp-drive paper treats superluminal motion as hypothetical and flags acceleration/propulsion as unresolved fundamental constraints, not just build details.
