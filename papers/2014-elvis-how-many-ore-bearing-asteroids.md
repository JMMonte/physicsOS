# How Many Ore-Bearing Asteroids?

## One-line summary

Elvis gives a conservative probabilistic screen for ore-bearing NEOs and estimates that PGM ore-bearing NEOs are rare, especially under stringent low-delta-v constraints.

## What it actually shows

- The paper distinguishes high concentration of a resource from commercial ore.
- For PGMs, it assumes Ni-Fe asteroids are the relevant parent population.
- The fraction of PGM-rich NEOs is reduced sharply by requiring large enough size and low enough delta-v.
- With \(D\gtrsim100\,\mathrm{m}\) and \(\Delta v<4.5\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\), the PGM ore-bearing fraction is estimated at roughly \(1/2000\) NEOs.
- Given about \(20000\) NEOs larger than \(100\,\mathrm{m}\) in the population estimate used, the paper estimates roughly \(10\) PGM ore-bearing NEOs.
- The estimate is conservative and highly uncertain; allowing \(\Delta v\) up to \(5.7\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\) increases the estimate by about an order of magnitude.

## Methods (briefly)

Analytic factorization of ore-bearing probability into abundance, accessibility, concentration, and value terms for two resource cases: PGMs and water.

## Key equations / results

- PGM ore-bearing NEOs, \(D\gtrsim100\,\mathrm{m}\), \(\Delta v<4.5\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\): \(\sim10\) objects.
- Sensitivity: \(\Delta v\) threshold dominates the count.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

The paper is a population-level screen, not a target validation. Its composition priors predate some later revisions to PGM grades and M-type asteroid interpretations.

## Caveats / open issues

- Does not identify specific mineable asteroids.
- Highly sensitive to survey completeness and composition classification.
- Economic ore status requires more than PGM presence: it requires recoverability and a market.

## How it informs the PGM target-readiness claim

This provides the base-rate warning for target selection: even if good PGM targets exist, they are expected to be rare among accessible NEOs.
