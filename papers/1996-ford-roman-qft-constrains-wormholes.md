# Quantum Field Theory Constrains Traversable Wormhole Geometries

## One-line summary

Ford and Roman use quantum inequalities to show that static macroscopic traversable wormholes are either near-Planckian or require negative energy concentrated in extremely thin regions.

## What it actually shows

- Traversable wormhole spacetimes require exotic stress-energy: weak-energy-condition violation, hence negative energy density for some observers.
- Quantum field theory allows local negative energy, but quantum inequalities constrain its magnitude-duration product.
- Applying those inequalities to static, spherically symmetric traversable wormholes makes macroscopic throats highly constrained.
- The result is not a no-go theorem for every conceivable quantum-gravity wormhole; it is a severe semiclassical constraint on the standard Morris-Thorne class.
- The paper treats no build process, no stabilization mechanism, and no experimental evidence for macroscopic negative-energy engineering.

## Methods (briefly)

The authors take a flat-spacetime quantum inequality for a minimally coupled massless scalar field, argue for its local use where curvature scales are large compared with sampling scales, and apply it to static wormhole stress-energy.

## Key equations / results

The load-bearing physical content is the quantum-inequality tradeoff: larger negative energy density can persist only for shorter proper time. Applied near a wormhole throat, this forces either Planck-scale structure or extreme length-scale separation.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

- Semiclassical QFT on a curved spacetime background.
- Static, spherically symmetric traversable wormholes.
- Local curvature scale large enough for flat-space quantum inequalities to apply.
- No closed timelike curves assumed in the analyzed spacetime.

## Caveats / open issues

The paper does not prove all wormholes impossible. It does show that "just engineer a wormhole" is not supported by known QFT: the necessary stress-energy is exotic and severely bounded.

## How it informs the FTL engineering claim

Wormholes are one of the standard routes invoked to evade local `v <= c`. This paper weighs against treating macroscopic traversable wormholes as an ordinary engineering problem.
