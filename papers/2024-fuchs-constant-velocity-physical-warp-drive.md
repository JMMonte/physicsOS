# Constant Velocity Physical Warp Drive Solution

## One-line summary

Fuchs et al. construct a constant-velocity subluminal warp-drive solution satisfying energy conditions; it is explicitly not an FTL result.

## What it actually shows

- A subluminal constant-velocity warp solution can be built from a stable matter shell plus a shift-vector distribution.
- The constructed solution satisfies NEC, WEC, DEC, and SEC for the analyzed case.
- The paper treats this as an initial step toward understanding physical warp solutions.
- Acceleration remains a major unresolved issue; naive acceleration introduces negative energy density.
- The Shapiro delay discussion distinguishes the solution from Alcubierre-style light-travel-time advances.
- The result supports subluminal spacetime engineering research, not FTL travel.

## Methods (briefly)

Numerical metric construction and energy-condition evaluation using the Warp Factory toolkit.

## Key equations / results

The relevant result is the existence of a positive-energy, constant-velocity, subluminal warp shell in the authors' numerical construction, with numerical error analysis.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

- Classical GR.
- Constant velocity only.
- Specific regular matter shell and shift-vector profile.
- Numerical finite-difference implementation.

## Caveats / open issues

The paper's popular reception often drops the word "subluminal." That word is load-bearing. This work may matter for relativistic propulsion or analog spacetime engineering, but it does not establish FTL.

## How it informs the FTL engineering claim

It is neutral-to-negative for the specific FTL claim: the newest positive-energy warp-drive result found in this pass is subluminal and still flags acceleration as unsolved.
