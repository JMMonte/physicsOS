# Impossibility of measuring faster-than-c signaling by the Scharnhorst effect

## One-line summary

Milonni and Svozil argue that the Scharnhorst effect cannot in principle yield a measured signal velocity larger than ordinary vacuum light speed.

## What it actually shows

- The Scharnhorst speed shift is not automatically an operational superluminal signalling channel.
- The paper directly targets the measurement/signalling interpretation, not the existence of the effective-QED correction itself.
- It is a limiting source for any claim that Casimir-vacuum FTL is already an engineering route.
- It is compatible with the weaker claim that constrained faster-than-c propagation laws can avoid the tachyonic-antitelephone problem.

## Methods (briefly)

Short theoretical argument about signal measurement and the physical preparation/readout constraints in the Scharnhorst setup.

## Key equations / results

The load-bearing result is the paper's conclusion that the Scharnhorst effect cannot give measured signal velocities above the ordinary vacuum value in an operational setup. No numerical value from the paper is used in the current audit.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

- Scharnhorst/Casimir parallel-plate setup.
- Operational measurement of signal velocity rather than formal phase/group velocity alone.

## Caveats / open issues

The paper does not analyze every possible effective-metric or preferred-frame FTL mechanism. It specifically cautions against overinterpreting the Scharnhorst effect.

## How it informs the mechanism-constrained FTL chronology claim

It is mixed evidence: it undercuts "humanity can use Scharnhorst for practical FTL," but it does not undercut the narrower causality point that constrained propagation laws can differ from arbitrary reciprocal tachyon rules.
