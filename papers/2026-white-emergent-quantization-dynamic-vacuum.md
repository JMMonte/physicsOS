# Emergent quantization from a dynamic vacuum

## One-line summary

A classical hydrodynamic ("Madelung-fluid") model of the vacuum, augmented with quadratic temporal dispersion, is shown to be **analytically isospectral** to the hydrogenic Coulomb problem after a parameter calibration to the Rydberg frequency.

## What it actually shows

- A mapping between (i) a classical compressible fluid with a specific dispersion relation and (ii) the eigenvalue problem of the hydrogen atom. The mapping is exact in the analytic sense (isospectral).
- That, given the calibration, no free parameters remain — i.e., once you fix the Rydberg scale, the rest of the hydrogenic spectrum follows.

## What it does **not** show (important)

- It does **not** show that the physical vacuum *is* such a medium. Mathematical equivalence between two equations is not evidence that one underlies the other; classical fluid analogs of quantum systems (Couder–Fort walking droplets, etc.) are a well-known mathematical curiosity, not a new physics.
- It does **not** demonstrate, prove, or even propose a mechanism for **extracting energy** from the vacuum. The paper is about reproducing a known spectrum, not about engines or energy budgets.
- It does **not** modify the standard derivations of the Casimir effect, nor argue that Casimir forces are non-conservative.
- It does **not** identify a new energy reservoir, scaling law, or thermodynamic cycle that would justify a continuous-power device.

## Methods (briefly)

Madelung's 1927 hydrodynamic reformulation of the Schrödinger equation is taken as the starting point. The authors add quadratic temporal dispersion to the dispersion relation of the fluid and derive the resulting eigenvalue equation. Calibration to the reduced-mass Rydberg fixes the model.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

- Non-relativistic, electrostatic Coulomb problem.
- A specific (chosen) dispersion relation, not derived from first principles.
- The mapping is at the level of the linear eigenvalue equation; nonlinear dynamics, field quantization, and many-body effects are not addressed.

## Caveats / open issues

- The marketing of this paper as "the theoretical foundation for why engineered Casimir cavities produce usable electrical energy" (per the Casimir, Inc. press release) is **not supported by the paper's contents**. The paper does not address Casimir cavities, energy extraction, or device performance at all.
- Active promotion by overunityresearch.com and e-catworld (a site historically associated with Andrea Rossi's "E-Cat" cold-fusion fraud) is a sociological red flag, not a physics judgment — but worth recording.
- The mainstream Casimir/QED community treats the Casimir effect as derivable without reference to zero-point energy at all (Jaffe 2005); a model built on "vacuum-as-medium" intuitions runs against this current.

## How it informs the Casimir Inc. claim

The press release uses this paper as theoretical cover. The paper does not provide that cover: nothing in it implies a continuous-power chip is consistent with thermodynamics. Treat the citation as **misdirection** until the company publishes a separate paper (a) deriving a non-conservative Casimir cycle and (b) reporting an experimental energy balance.
