# Precious and Structural Metals on Asteroids

## One-line summary

Cannon et al. update asteroid metal-resource estimates using meteorite data and find that PGMs are the main plausible return-to-Earth metal class, but realistic grades and beneficiation matter.

## What it actually shows

- The paper compares asteroid-relevant meteorite compositions against terrestrial ore grades for many elements.
- Only a small subset of elements reach or exceed terrestrial ore-grade concentrations in plausible asteroid materials.
- PGMs remain the strongest candidate for return-to-Earth metals because they combine high value, comparatively high asteroid grades, and small terrestrial production volumes.
- Total PGM concentrations in iron meteorites are about \(6\text{--}230\,\mathrm{ppm}\), lower than some older claims based on extrapolating chondritic ratios.
- The \(230\,\mathrm{ppm}\) high end is an upper expectation for metal-rich material, not a guarantee for remotely classified M/X-type asteroids.
- Modern interpretations of M-type/X-complex bodies allow mixtures of metal and silicates rather than monolithic iron bodies.
- Beneficiation is first-order: the economic payload must be refined PGMs or a high-grade concentrate, not undifferentiated asteroid bulk.

## Methods (briefly)

Compilation and statistical treatment of meteorite PGM measurements, comparison against terrestrial deposits, and discussion of mineral-processing strategies using metal grains, refractory metal nuggets, and bulk metal-rich material.

## Key equations / results

- Iron meteorite total PGM range: \(\sim6\text{--}230\,\mathrm{ppm}\).
- Percentiles for total PGM grade in iron meteorites include median \(\sim40.78\,\mathrm{ppm}\), 80th percentile \(\sim91.23\,\mathrm{ppm}\), and 90th percentile \(\sim107.86\,\mathrm{ppm}\).
- The authors caution that these grades assume metal-rich material and may be upper values for real asteroids with mixed silicate-metal compositions.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

Meteorites are proxies for asteroid materials. They do not identify a specific mineable NEA, nor do they solve sampling bias, regolith heterogeneity, or remote-sensing ambiguity.

## Caveats / open issues

- No specific target is validated as economically mineable.
- Remote M/X classification is insufficient to determine PGM grade.
- Processing micron-scale refractory metal nuggets may be technically difficult.

## How it informs the PGM target-readiness claim

This paper sets the ore-grade prior for a target screen: even a highly metal-rich asteroid must be processed or beneficiated before returned material has terrestrial PGM-market value.
