# Rotating Casimir systems

## One-line summary

Proposes a *zero-point–driven* device whose ground state corresponds to permanent rotation, then argues this is consistent with thermodynamics specifically because **the device produces no useful work** ("perpetuum mobile of the fourth kind").

## What it actually shows

- For certain Casimir-coupled rotating geometries (with magnetic fields, doped nanotubes), the lowest-energy state has nonzero angular momentum.
- This is not a thermodynamic violation because the rotation is the equilibrium state itself — no entropy decrease, no heat flowing from cold to hot, no work delivered to an external load.
- Crucially: any attempt to *extract work* from this rotation immediately recovers the standard prohibitions. The motion is real but the engine is not.

## How it informs the Casimir Inc. claim

This is the closest the serious literature gets to "Casimir-powered perpetual motion," and even here the author is explicit: **no work is delivered**. A chip advertised as supplying 1.5 V × 25 μA to an external circuit indefinitely is in a totally different category — it *is* claiming continuous work output from a static cavity at thermal equilibrium with its environment. That sits squarely against the second law.
