# Generic warp drives violate the null energy condition

## One-line summary

Santiago, Schuster, and Visser argue that physically reasonable warp drives generically violate the null energy condition, including cases advertised as positive-energy.

## What it actually shows

- The paper responds directly to recent claims that positive-energy warp drives remove exotic-matter barriers.
- Positive energy density for one observer family is insufficient: the weak energy condition must hold for all timelike observers.
- Their analysis finds that physically reasonable warp drives violate the null energy condition and therefore also the weak, strong, and dominant energy conditions.
- Within standard GR, the authors treat energy-condition violation as unavoidable for warp drives.
- In modified gravity, they argue that analogous geometric convergence conditions still impose strong constraints.

## Methods (briefly)

General stress-energy and energy-condition analysis for warp-drive spacetimes, with attention to observer dependence of energy density.

## Key equations / results

The key result for this claim is not a single number but the observer-invariant energy-condition conclusion: satisfying energy density for comoving/Eulerian observers does not establish WEC/NEC satisfaction.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

- Classical GR for the strongest claim.
- "Physically reasonable" warp-drive conditions as defined in the paper.
- Modified-gravity comments are constraint arguments, not a full theorem for every alternative theory.

## Caveats / open issues

The paper does not prove that all mathematically definable spacetime engineering is impossible. It targets warp drives as usually proposed and the specific recent positive-energy narrative.

## How it informs the FTL engineering claim

This is load-bearing negative evidence. It directly rebuts the claim that positive-energy warp-drive papers have converted superluminal travel into an engineering problem.
