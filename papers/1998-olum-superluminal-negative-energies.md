# Superluminal travel requires negative energies

## One-line summary

Olum gives a definition of superluminal travel in a general spacetime and proves, under a generic condition, that it requires weak-energy-condition violation.

## What it actually shows

- Defining FTL in arbitrary curved spacetime is subtle; some metrics that look superluminal are coordinate artifacts.
- Olum's operational definition compares a path to neighboring paths reaching a destination surface.
- Under that definition, and assuming the generic condition, superluminal travel requires weak-energy-condition violation.
- Weak-energy-condition violation means negative energy density for at least some timelike observers.
- The theorem targets global superluminal travel, not merely local group velocities or coordinate effects.

## Methods (briefly)

The paper formulates a spacetime-geometric definition of "superluminal travel" and uses focusing/energy-condition arguments to connect superluminality with negative energy.

## Key equations / results

The central result is theorem-level rather than a numerical equation: superluminal travel, as defined operationally by arrival earlier than neighboring signal paths, requires violation of the weak energy condition.

## Assumptions and regime of validity

- Classical GR plus standard energy-condition language.
- Olum's definition of superluminal travel.
- Generic condition assumed.
- Does not decide what quantum gravity might allow outside semiclassical GR.

## Caveats / open issues

The proof does not rule out every semantic use of "faster than light" such as anomalous group velocities or benign effective-metric effects. It directly hits macroscopic travel/communication proposals that aim to beat nearby causal paths.

## How it informs the FTL engineering claim

This is load-bearing evidence against the claim. It converts "FTL travel" from a propulsion-design problem into a requirement for stress-energy outside ordinary positive-energy matter.
