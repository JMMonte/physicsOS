# Pinto's Casimir engine patents (1999–2003)

## One-line summary

Fabrizio Pinto holds a family of US patents describing Casimir-force engines that rely on **tuning the permittivity** (or other boundary properties) of at least one plate during the cycle in order to render the Casimir force non-conservative and net positive work.

## What they actually claim

- The bare Casimir force between fixed boundaries is conservative — a closed cycle nets zero work. Patents acknowledge this.
- Their core mechanism: modulate a plate property (refractive index, conductivity, geometry) during the cycle, so the force at "approach" differs from the force at "separation". The path integral around the loop in property-space can then be nonzero.

## What they do not show

- Patents do not establish experimental net-energy gain. Pinto's 1999 paper computes a theoretical upper bound; no replicated experiment has demonstrated useful power output in the 27 years since.
- The energy required to drive the permittivity modulation is generally larger than (or comparable to) any extracted Casimir work, in any realistic candidate material — this is well-known in the niche literature and is the standard reason these schemes do not commercialize.

## How it informs the Casimir Inc. claim

This is the prior art Casimir Inc. is almost certainly leaning on (though the press release does not cite Pinto). Two implications:

1. The *theoretical loophole* (non-conservative cycle via property modulation) is real and patent-protected. It is not magic.
2. The *practical hurdle* (driving the modulation costs more energy than is extracted) is what has stopped this idea from becoming a device for nearly three decades. The press release does not address it.

A credible Casimir Inc. would publish: (a) the modulation mechanism, (b) the energy ledger of the modulation drive, (c) the demonstrated net efficiency. None of this is in the public record as of 2026-05-13.
